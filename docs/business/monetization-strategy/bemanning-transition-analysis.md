# DriverNord — Bemanning/Staffing Transition Analysis

**Date:** 2026-05-14  
**Purpose:** Analyze when and whether DriverNord should move into bemanning (staffing), and what the transition path looks like.  
**Governing rule:** No cold outreach. No deployment. No legal conclusions stronger than the evidence supports. All legal assessments marked [LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED].

---

## What Bemanning Would Unlock

Bemanning (staffing) means DriverNord employs drivers and bills client companies an hourly rate that includes a markup above the driver's direct employment cost. The key difference from the placement model:

| Dimension | Placement (Model A — current) | Bemanning (Model C — future) |
|-----------|------------------------------|------------------------------|
| Who employs the driver | Client company | DriverNord |
| Revenue logic | One-time fee per hire | Ongoing hourly billing × all hours worked |
| Revenue per driver/year | 35,000–45,000 SEK (once) | ~220,000–259,000 SEK/year ongoing (markup on ~450 SEK/h bill rate) |
| Revenue stability | Lumpy (depends on hire events) | Recurring (depends on hours billed) |
| Gross margin | 60–85% | 15–25% net |
| Driver count needed for 1 MSEK/month revenue | 22–28 placements/month | 4–5 full-time staffed drivers |
| Capital requirement | Minimal | HIGH (30–60 day payroll float) |
| Employer liability | None | Full (accidents, sick leave, disputes) |

**The financial case for bemanning:** Simplex Bemanning generates 389 MSEK/year with 555 employees. The revenue-per-staffed-driver is approximately 700,000 SEK/year. At 40 staffed CE drivers, DriverNord could generate 28 MSEK/year. This is a very large number relative to placement-fee revenue.

**STRONG INFERENCE.** The absolute revenue ceiling of bemanning is much higher than placement. But so is the capital, complexity, and risk floor.

## Why Bemanning Is Financially Attractive

### The math on one staffed driver

| Cost component | Calculation | Annual cost |
|---------------|-------------|-------------|
| CE driver base wage (Transportavtalet) | ~215 SEK/h × 1,850 h | 397,750 SEK |
| Arbetsgivaravgift (31.42%) | 397,750 × 31.42% | 124,972 SEK |
| Semesterlön (12%) | 397,750 × 12% | 47,730 SEK |
| Sick/pension/Fora insurance | ~10% of wage | 39,775 SEK |
| **Total direct employment cost** | | **~610,227 SEK/year** |

Bill rate to client: 450 SEK/h × 1,850h = **832,500 SEK/year**  
Gross profit per driver: 832,500 - 610,227 = **222,273 SEK/year**  
Gross margin on bill rate: **~26.7%**

After administration, management, and overhead, net margin falls to 15–20% — consistent with Simplex's publicly reported 2.98% net margin and Lernia's 0.8%.

**VERIFIED FACT** (from public Allabolag financial data and SV Bemanning price benchmarks).

### Recurring vs. lumpy revenue

At 40,000 SEK per placement and 2 placements/month, DriverNord earns 80,000 SEK/month from placement fees.

At 450 SEK/h with 10 staffed drivers working full time, DriverNord bills 45 × 1,850h/12 months = ~693,750 SEK/month. With 26.7% gross margin → ~185,250 SEK/month gross — from just 10 drivers.

This comparison makes bemanning look compelling. But the working capital requirement changes everything.

## Why Bemanning Is Operationally Harder

### 1. Payroll float

DriverNord must pay drivers on their normal salary schedule (typically monthly) whether or not client companies have paid their invoices. Standard B2B payment terms in Sweden are 30 days. With 10 staffed drivers at ~50,000 SEK/month each → 500,000 SEK in monthly payroll that DriverNord must float before receiving client payment.

With 40 staffed drivers → 2,000,000 SEK monthly payroll float.

**This is working capital, not profit.** It must be funded from equity, a credit line, or factoring (which costs 1–3% per invoice).

### 2. Kollektivavtal (collective agreement)

Transport staffing companies in Sweden must adhere to the transport sector's Kollektivavtal — specifically the agreement between Transportarbetsgivarna and Svenska Transportarbetareförbundet (Transport). This covers:
- Minimum wage rates (locked to Transportavtalet rates)
- Hours of work and rest requirements (EG/EU transport regulations)
- Sick pay obligations (Sjuklönelagen)
- Pension contributions (Fora)
- Holiday pay (Semesterlagen)

Failure to adhere is a serious legal and reputational risk. [LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED — a Swedish employment lawyer familiar with transport sector Kollektivavtal is essential before any employment of drivers.]

### 3. Bemanningslag compliance (2023 reform implications)

The 2023 Swedish reform (Lag om uthyrning av arbetstagare) strengthened protections for staffed workers. Key requirements for a bemanning company:
- The staffed worker must receive equal pay and conditions to the client company's direct employees in the same role
- Client companies may only use staffed workers for temporary needs, not to permanently replace direct employment
- The staffed worker has right to direct employment at the client after 24 months of consecutive assignment

This reform makes short-term staffing acceptable but creates pressure for conversion to direct employment at the 24-month mark. [LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED — the interpretation of "consecutive assignment" and the 24-month rule requires qualified legal analysis.]

### 4. Insurance

Transport sector employers must register with Fora (Swedish insurance collective):
- Tjänstepension (occupational pension)
- Avtalsgruppsförsäkring (disability/life insurance)
- Trygghetsrådet (redeployment insurance)
- Fora's mandatory insurance registry

Cost: approximately 7–12% of wage cost. Additional liability insurance for transport-specific risk (accidents, cargo damage, third-party liability) may also be required for the employer. [LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED — exact Fora obligations depend on the specific Kollektivavtal applicable.]

### 5. Driver driving time compliance

CE/C/D drivers are subject to EU driving time regulations (EC 561/2006) — maximum 9 hours per day, 56 hours per week, mandatory rest periods, tachograph compliance. As employer, DriverNord would be legally responsible for ensuring these are not violated. This requires tachograph monitoring software and compliance expertise.

## Working Capital Requirement

| Scenario | Staffed drivers | Monthly payroll | Required float | Funding source |
|----------|----------------|----------------|----------------|----------------|
| Minimum viable | 5 drivers | 250,000 SEK/month | 500,000–750,000 SEK | Seed capital or credit line |
| Small scale | 20 drivers | 1,000,000 SEK/month | 2,000,000–3,000,000 SEK | Series A or bank credit |
| Viable at scale | 50 drivers | 2,500,000 SEK/month | 5,000,000–7,500,000 SEK | Institutional credit |

**The 500,000–750,000 SEK minimum float requirement** is a hard capital gate. For a pre-revenue startup without a credit line, this is a significant barrier. DriverNord must generate sufficient placement-fee revenue to fund this float or raise external capital before entering bemanning.

**STRONG INFERENCE:** Placement revenue of at least 500,000 SEK/month (sustained) would provide enough cash flow to consider funding a small bemanning operation. This implies 10–14 shortlist sales per month or 12+ success fees per month — achievable only with a substantial driver database and established client relationships.

## Hybrid Model Options

Instead of building bemanning internally, DriverNord has four structural options:

### Option 1: DriverNord as Lead/Recruitment Engine Only

DriverNord continues as a pure placement business. Never enters bemanning.

**Pros:**
- Zero employment liability
- 60–85% gross margin maintained
- Scales without capital
- Model A is already proven (in design); simply requires execution

**Cons:**
- Revenue ceiling is limited by number of hire events
- Does not capture the ongoing staffing revenue
- Larger transport companies often want staffing, not placement

**Verdict:** Best path for the next 18–24 months. Do not abandon this too early.

---

### Option 2: DriverNord + Staffing Partner

DriverNord finds, qualifies, and introduces drivers. An existing licensed bemanning operator (e.g., a smaller regional staffing company) employs and invoices. DriverNord earns a referral or introduction fee from the staffing partner (or from the client) for each driver they staff.

**Pros:**
- Zero employment liability for DriverNord
- No payroll float required
- Revenue from the staffing volume without the operational overhead
- Fast to implement (weeks, not months)
- Can be structured as a referral fee (5,000–15,000 SEK per staffed driver introduced) or a revenue share (10–15% of ongoing bill rate)

**Cons:**
- DriverNord earns less per driver than operating staffing directly
- Dependent on partner quality and reliability
- Brand exposure if partner treats drivers poorly

**Verdict:** RECOMMENDED for Phase 3–4 as a bridge. Partner with an existing bemanning operator to capture some staffing revenue without the capital requirement. This also validates demand before DriverNord commits to building bemanning internally.

---

### Option 3: DriverNord as Bemanning Operator (Later)

DriverNord builds its own staffing operation — obtains Kollektivavtal, registers with Fora, employs drivers, bills clients directly.

**Pros:**
- Maximum revenue per driver
- Full control of driver experience
- Access to the 200+ MSEK/year market that Simplex operates in

**Cons:**
- Capital-intensive (working capital float)
- Legal complexity (employment law, Kollektivavtal, Bemanningslag)
- Operational complexity (payroll, compliance, dispute management)
- Timeline: 6–12 months from decision to first staffed driver

**Verdict:** Phase 7; only after Phase 6 assessment confirms viability and capital is secured.

---

### Option 4: DriverNord as AI Infrastructure Provider to Staffing Companies

DriverNord licenses its driver matching, scoring, and intake platform to existing staffing companies. Staffing companies pay for the technology; DriverNord does not employ anyone.

**Pros:**
- Zero employment liability
- High margin technology licensing revenue
- Can scale to multiple staffing company clients
- Norway and European expansion path without local employment compliance

**Cons:**
- Requires white-label version of the platform (engineering investment)
- Creates competition with DriverNord's own placement business
- Staffing companies may not want an external technology vendor with insight into their driver pool

**Verdict:** Phase 5–6 diversification strategy. Not the primary path but worth developing as a parallel revenue stream.

---

## Clear Recommendation

### Do not enter bemanning now.

**Reasons:**
1. No capital for payroll float (minimum 500,000–750,000 SEK required for even 5 staffed drivers)
2. No Kollektivavtal (requires negotiation or adoption of an existing agreement — months of work)
3. No Fora insurance registration (required before employing anyone)
4. No placement revenue to fund the transition
5. No driver database to staff from
6. The placement model is not yet proven — building on top of an unproven model adds risk to risk

### Prepare for bemanning in Phase 5.

**Actions to take in Phase 3–4 (can be AI-driven):**
- Research existing transport sector bemanning operators who might be partnership candidates
- Draft a working capital model for 5, 20, and 50 staffed drivers
- Identify the specific Kollektivavtal applicable to transport staffing and document its key terms
- Map Fora insurance costs into the unit economics model
- Identify which current client companies would prefer staffing over placement (this data will emerge naturally as client relationships develop)

### Partner first in Phase 3.

When DriverNord identifies a client who wants staffing rather than placement, the short-term solution is referral to an existing bemanning partner. This generates some revenue (referral fee or revenue share), builds the client relationship, and validates staffing demand — without the capital and legal overhead of operating staffing directly.
