# DriverNord — B2B Barrier-to-Entry Research Framework

*Created: 2026-05-10*
*Phase: 1.5 — B2B Research (active)*
*Scope: Research strategy only. No code. No outreach yet.*

---

## 1. Research Objective

Before building a company intake form or making any B2B contact, DriverNord needs to answer one question:

> **Which Swedish transport companies can we realistically sign as a pilot client within 30–60 days?**

This is not the same as asking "which companies need drivers?" Every transport company in Sweden needs drivers. The shortage is structural. The differentiating question is:

> **Which companies with a driver need can we actually reach, convince, and onboard — given DriverNord's current stage, resources, and lack of track record?**

### The four variables that determine pilot feasibility

| Variable | What it measures | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Visible driver need** | Are they actively hiring C/CE/D drivers now? | No need = no urgency = no pilot |
| **Low barrier to entry** | Can we become a vendor without a 6-month approval process? | High barriers mean quarters of delay |
| **Accessible decision-maker** | Can we talk to the person who can say yes? | No access = no deal, regardless of fit |
| **Pilot feasibility** | Can we deliver value in 30–60 days with current infrastructure? | Overpromising early destroys credibility |

### What DriverNord can deliver today

Before approaching any company, be precise about the current offer:

- Qualified driver profiles with license (C/CE/D), YKB status, driver card, domain, availability, and shift preference — captured digitally, structured, and scored
- A ranked shortlist matched against the company's specific need (license, domain, region, shift, urgency)
- Human-governed contact flow — DriverNord suggests contact, recruiter approves, message goes to driver
- Simulation mode only until `SMS_PROVIDER` credentials are configured — real messaging requires one additional setup step

**What DriverNord cannot deliver today:**

- Employment or staffing (DriverNord is not a bemanning company)
- Guaranteed placements or fill rates (no operational history)
- A large existing driver pool (pool is being built)
- Post-placement management (shifts, attendance, contracts)

Pilot framing: "We will build a qualified shortlist of C/CE drivers matched to your specific need and present them to you. You decide who to contact. If you hire one, we have proven the system works."

## 2. Target Company Segments

### Segment A — SME Transport Companies (5–50 trucks)

**Definition:** Owner-operated or family-run transport companies with a small fleet and recurring driver needs. Typical Swedish examples: regional tipping operators, distribution SMEs, local cold-chain companies.

**Why it matters:** The decision-maker is the owner or transport manager and can say yes in a single conversation. No procurement process. No vendor panel. No code of conduct review. These companies feel the driver shortage most acutely because they cannot absorb it through internal HR capacity.

**Expected driver need:** High and chronic. Ongoing C/CE demand. Often hire through Jobzone, Rekryteringsgruppen, or directly through Arbetsförmedlingen.

**Barrier level:** Low. Entry typically requires only: a direct conversation, a simple email agreement, and a clear pilot scope.

**Pilot feasibility:** High. Owner can agree in one meeting. Can start within 1–2 weeks of agreement. Results visible within 30 days.

**Recommended priority:** 1 — Primary pilot target.

---

### Segment B — Regional Transport Operators (50–200 trucks)

**Definition:** Mid-size operators with regional footprint (Stockholm, Uppsala, Sörmland, Örebro, Mälardalen). Have a transport manager or operations director. May have basic HR function but no formal vendor panel.

**Why it matters:** Larger driver volume than SMEs. More hiring signal. Still accessible without a procurement department. A win here produces stronger proof of concept than an SME win.

**Expected driver need:** High. Recurring CE/C demand, often by domain (distribution, long-haul, refrigerated).

**Barrier level:** Low to medium. Decision likely goes through transport manager or operations director rather than owner. May require a simple service agreement or brief process explanation.

**Pilot feasibility:** Medium-high. 2–4 week setup. May require brief internal approval before starting.

**Recommended priority:** 2 — Secondary pilot target after SME wins.

---

### Segment C — Mid-Size Logistics Companies (200–1,000 employees)

**Definition:** Companies with an HR function, possibly a dedicated recruitment manager. Examples: regional distribution networks, specialized cold-chain logistics, national courier operations.

**Why it matters:** Higher volume. More systematic hiring process. A DriverNord match here is more demonstrable as a scalable product.

**Expected driver need:** Moderate to high. Often already using staffing agencies. May have framework agreements with Jobzone, Tranpenad, or similar.

**Barrier level:** Medium. HR involvement adds a layer. May require a data processor agreement (GDPR), reference from another client, or brief procurement review.

**Pilot feasibility:** Medium. 4–8 week sales cycle. More questions. More documentation needed.

**Recommended priority:** 3 — Target after SME and regional operator pilots exist.

---

### Segment D — Companies Using Staffing/Bemanning Agencies

**Definition:** Any company from segments A–C that is currently using a bemanning company for driver acquisition. Identifiable by: job ads run under a staffing agency name, Linkedin posts by agency recruiters naming the client, or direct company statement.

**Why it matters:** These companies have already decided to outsource driver acquisition. They understand the concept. They are paying for a service. The question is whether DriverNord can supplement, replace, or feed into their existing flow.

**Two sub-strategies:**

1. **Direct approach:** "We can deliver pre-qualified driver profiles for a fraction of what you pay the agency — and you keep full control of who you contact."
2. **Agency approach:** Position DriverNord as a supplier to the staffing agency itself. The agency gets a better qualified driver pool. DriverNord avoids employer-direct complexity.

**Barrier level:** Variable. Companies using agencies may have exclusivity clauses. Check before approaching.

**Pilot feasibility:** Medium. The concept is proven to them; the trust in a new actor is the barrier.

**Recommended priority:** 3 (direct) / 2 (via agency partnership).

---

### Segment E — Companies Repeatedly Posting Driver Job Ads

**Definition:** Companies running repeated ads for the same driver role across multiple weeks or months. Identifiable on Arbetsförmedlingen, Blocket Jobb, LinkedIn, and company career pages.

**Why it matters:** Repeated posting is the single strongest signal of unresolved urgent need. They have tried and failed. They are spending money on ads. They are open to alternatives.

**Cross-segment tag:** This is a qualifying signal, not a segment. Apply to all segments above to elevate priority.

**Detection method:** Search the same company name on Arbetsförmedlingen across a 4-week window. Multiple ads for the same role = strong urgency signal.

**Recommended priority:** Elevate any company from segments A–C by one priority level if this tag applies.

---

### Segment F — Large Enterprise Logistics Groups

**Definition:** PostNord, DHL, Bring, DB Schenker, Boliden, Martin & Servera, Dagab, and similar groups with 1,000+ employees, national/international operations, and structured procurement.

**Why it matters:** These are long-term strategic accounts and competitive benchmarks. They validate the market and define the ceiling for what DriverNord can eventually become.

**Expected driver need:** Very high. These are among the largest Swedish employers of professional drivers.

**Barrier level:** Very high. Formal supplier approval processes. Vendor panels. Code of conduct requirements. GDPR/data processor review. Insurance requirements. Central procurement, not accessible via a single transport manager.

**Pilot feasibility:** Low in the short term. 6–18 month procurement cycles are normal. However: some large groups have regional operations or subsidiary companies with more local decision-making. This is the entry point to research — not the group procurement, but the depot or regional manager.

**Recommended priority:** 5 — Research as benchmark only. Do not pursue as immediate pilot target. Revisit after 2–3 SME pilot wins.

## 3. Barrier Types

Every transport company DriverNord might approach has one or more of the following barriers. Identifying the relevant barriers before contact saves time and allows the approach to be calibrated.

### 3.1 Supplier Approval Process

**What it is:** A formal process to become a registered vendor. Common in companies with >200 employees. May include: credit check, business registration verification, insurance certificate, GDPR review, code of conduct signature.

**Detection:** Company website → footer → "For suppliers" or "Leverantörer" or "Become a partner." Procurement portals are also a signal.

**DriverNord impact:** DriverNord is not a staffing company, not an employer of record, and not a technology platform vendor in the traditional sense. The right category is "recruitment services" or "driver acquisition services." This framing may or may not exist in the procurement system.

**Mitigation:** Start with companies that have no visible supplier portal. If a portal exists, determine if a pilot can begin informally before formal registration.

---

### 3.2 Procurement Portal

**What it is:** A self-service vendor registration system (e.g., Mercell, Visma Proceedo, Ivalua, Coupa). Common at large enterprises and some public-sector-adjacent companies (PostNord is partly state-owned).

**Detection:** "Registrera dig som leverantör" or "Leverantörsportal" on company website. Job ads that route through a named procurement system.

**DriverNord impact:** Procurement portals typically require weeks to months to complete. They assume a known vendor category. DriverNord does not fit neatly into a standard category, which can cause delays.

**Mitigation:** Avoid procurement-portal companies as pilot targets. Research them for category intelligence only.

---

### 3.3 Vendor Code of Conduct

**What it is:** A written standard that vendors must sign, covering: labor practices, anti-corruption, environmental policy, GDPR, subcontractor standards.

**Detection:** "Code of Conduct" or "Uppförandekod" on supplier/vendor page.

**DriverNord impact:** Signing a code of conduct is low-friction. The main risk is that DriverNord's driver data handling needs to be explicitly described when signing. Legal review of current policies would be required before signing any code of conduct.

**Mitigation:** Acceptable to sign with SME-friendly code of conduct equivalents. For enterprise accounts, defer until legal pages are finalized and reviewed.

---

### 3.4 GDPR / Data Processor Agreement

**What it is:** A written agreement (DPA) between DriverNord as data processor and the company as data controller, or vice versa, depending on how driver data flows.

**Detection:** Companies with any data-aware supplier requirements will raise this. HR departments routinely ask for it.

**DriverNord impact:** When DriverNord shares a shortlist of driver profiles (name, phone, license, YKB) with a transport company, data is shared. A DPA is required before this can happen at any regulated company. DriverNord's legal pages are currently "Preliminär version" — not yet legally reviewed.

**Mitigation:** For initial pilots: share only anonymized or aggregate profile information (score, license class, availability, domain) until a simple DPA is in place. Full PII transfer (name, phone) requires a DPA. Prepare a simple bilateral DPA template before approaching segment C or above.

**Priority action:** Before any real data sharing with a company, have a 1-page DPA template prepared (can be simple for SME; more complex for mid-size and above).

---

### 3.5 Insurance Requirements

**What it is:** Some companies require vendors to carry professional indemnity insurance, liability insurance, or sector-specific coverage.

**Detection:** Supplier requirements page. Rare at SME level; more common at mid-size and above.

**DriverNord impact:** DriverNord does not employ drivers and does not place drivers — it provides acquisition and matching infrastructure. Professional indemnity for incorrect matching or data misuse may still be relevant.

**Mitigation:** At SME pilot stage, insurance is unlikely to be raised. Note as a requirement for segment C and above. Research appropriate coverage before pursuing mid-size accounts.

---

### 3.6 Financial Stability Requirements

**What it is:** Credit check, audited accounts, or minimum revenue thresholds required by some companies before onboarding a vendor.

**Detection:** Common in public procurement and large enterprise supplier portals. Rare at SME.

**DriverNord impact:** DriverNord is pre-revenue, pre-pilot. This requirement cannot currently be met at scale.

**Mitigation:** Start only with companies that do not require financial verification. Avoid public procurement paths entirely.

---

### 3.7 References / Case Studies Required

**What it is:** Some companies will not trial a new vendor without a reference from a known client or a published case study.

**Detection:** "Can you provide client references?" in initial conversations. Required for segment C and above.

**DriverNord impact:** No references exist at pilot stage. This is a chicken-and-egg problem — the first pilot must be won without references.

**Mitigation:** Frame the first pilot explicitly as a reference-generating exercise. Offer favorable terms (first shortlist free, no fee unless a driver is contacted) in exchange for permission to use the company as a named reference. Get this in writing at the start.

---

### 3.8 Existing HR/Vendor Panels

**What it is:** A pre-approved list of staffing and recruitment vendors that the company is contracted to use. Exclusivity or preference clauses may apply.

**Detection:** "We already work with [Jobzone/Rekryteringsgruppen/Tranpenad]" in initial conversations. Framework agreements with bemanning companies.

**DriverNord impact:** DriverNord is not a bemanning company and does not compete directly with the panel vendor for employment liability. However, budget and procurement relationship may still be controlled by the panel.

**Mitigation:** Position as complementary infrastructure, not a replacement. "Your current agency handles the employment relationship. We handle the qualified lead flow to both you and them." This reduces direct competition with existing panel vendors.

---

### 3.9 Decision-Maker Access

**What it is:** The ability to reach the person who can approve a pilot without routing through gatekeepers.

**Detection:** At SME level, the owner is often publicly listed on company website or LinkedIn. At regional operator level, the transport manager is usually named. At mid-size companies, access goes through HR or a procurement gatekeeper first.

**DriverNord impact:** No access = no deal. Identify the decision-maker role before investing time in the approach.

**Decision-maker hierarchy by segment:**
- SME (5–50 trucks): Owner / VD (CEO) / Transport Manager
- Regional operator: Transport Manager / Operations Director
- Mid-size logistics: HR Manager / Recruitment Manager (gatekeeper) → Operations Director (decision-maker)
- Large enterprise: Central procurement → category manager (not accessible at pilot stage)

---

### 3.10 Trust Barrier

**What it is:** A company's unwillingness to share driver hiring needs with a new, unknown actor — especially one with no references, no brand recognition, and no track record.

**Detection:** Expressed as skepticism: "How do we know your drivers are real?" or "We've never heard of DriverNord." Common at all segments; stronger at mid-size and above.

**Mitigation:** Lower the perceived risk of the first step. Do not ask for a contract commitment. Do not ask for payment. Ask for: "Let us run one test match against your current open need. We present you the shortlist. If it's useful, we discuss next steps. No cost, no commitment."

---

### 3.11 Staffing Agency / Bemanning Confusion

**What it is:** The most common misclassification barrier. Companies assume DriverNord is another bemanning company — which means they compare on employment liability, kollektivavtal, insurance, fill-rate guarantees, and price-per-hour.

**Detection:** "Are you a staffing agency?" or "Do you employ the drivers?"

**Mitigation:** Address immediately and explicitly:
> "DriverNord is not a bemanning company. We do not employ drivers. We build the qualified shortlist of drivers who match your exact requirements — license, YKB, domain, availability — and you contact them directly or through your existing agency. You own the relationship."

This positions DriverNord as infrastructure/tooling, not competition with existing bemanning relationships.

## 4. Large Account Strategy

### 4.1 Classification

Large enterprise logistics accounts should be classified before any research effort is spent:

| Classification | Criteria | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Research benchmark | >500 employees, formal procurement, no visible SME entry | Document supplier requirements; use as market standard reference. No outreach. |
| Long-term target (12–24 months) | >200 employees, procurement process, no current entry path | Research and track. Revisit after 3+ pilot wins. |
| Medium-term target (6–12 months) | Regional subsidiary with local decision-making, no group procurement | Identify local depot/branch contact. Approach after first pilot win. |
| Immediate target | Clear low-barrier entry despite size | Evaluate on pilot feasibility score. Treat as Segment A or B. |

### 4.2 Named Large Account Research

The following large accounts should be researched for supplier requirements, not approached as pilot targets. The research output informs future strategy and helps identify any local operational entry points that bypass central procurement.

---

**PostNord AB**

- Swedish state-owned postal and logistics group. Among the largest employers of professional drivers in Sweden.
- Supplier portal: likely via Visma or similar (search "leverantör PostNord" or check corporate.postnord.com).
- GDPR: Extensive. PostNord processes significant personal data volumes. Any vendor sharing driver data will require a full DPA.
- Entry path to research: PostNord operates regional sorting and distribution depots with local transport operations. Depot managers may have more local hiring discretion. Research whether depot-level hiring bypasses group procurement.
- Collective agreement: Bound by Transport Collective Agreement (Transportavtalet). Driver placement must comply.
- Classification: Research benchmark. Long-term target if depot-level entry confirmed.

---

**DHL Express / DHL Freight / DHL Supply Chain (Sweden)**

- German-owned global logistics group. Sweden operations across express, freight, and supply chain.
- Supplier onboarding: DHL has a global supplier portal. Swedish operations may have local onboarding paths for smaller contracts.
- Code of conduct: DHL Supplier Code of Conduct is publicly available. Covers labor, environment, anti-corruption.
- GDPR: DHL is GDPR-compliant by policy. Will require DPA for any driver data sharing.
- Entry path to research: DHL Freight (long-haul, CE) and DHL Supply Chain (warehouse distribution) have regional Swedish operations. Research regional operations manager contact at depot level.
- Classification: Research benchmark. Long-term target (DHL Freight regional, specifically).

---

**Bring Logistics (Sweden)**

- Norwegian-owned (Posten Norge). Operates in Sweden under Bring brand. Significant parcel and B2B freight volume.
- Supplier requirements: Research via bring.se/en/about-bring/supplier-information. Has supplier code of conduct.
- GDPR: Nordic group, GDPR-compliant. Will require DPA.
- Entry path to research: Bring has Swedish distribution centers (Stockholm, Göteborg, Malmö). Regional terminal managers may have local hiring authority for driver needs.
- Classification: Research benchmark. Medium-term target if terminal-level entry confirmed.

---

**DB Schenker (Sweden)**

- German state railway logistics subsidiary. Significant Swedish freight and distribution operations (Schenker AB Sweden).
- Supplier portal: DB Schenker uses a global supplier management system. Swedish local contact may be accessible for smaller contracts via schenker.com/se.
- Code of conduct: Published. Standard sustainability and labor requirements.
- GDPR: Full compliance requirements. DPA required.
- Entry path to research: DB Schenker Sweden has regional depots. Research depot transport coordinator or regional operations manager.
- Classification: Research benchmark. Long-term target (regional depot entry only).

---

**Key insight on all four large accounts:** Central procurement at these groups is 6–18 months. The research question is not "how do we get approved by central procurement" but "does a regional depot or subsidiary have local hiring authority that bypasses central procurement, and if so, who is that person?"

## 5. Barrier Scoring Model

### 5.1 Pilot Feasibility Score (0–100)

Score each company across 7 dimensions. Sum the scores. Higher score = lower barrier = higher pilot priority.

| Dimension | Max Points | Scoring Guide |
|---|---|---|
| **Visible driver need** | 25 | 25 = active job ad for C/CE/D now; 15 = recently posted (last 30 days); 8 = general driver hiring mentioned on website; 0 = no signal |
| **Stockholm / regional fit** | 15 | 15 = Stockholm city or immediate region; 10 = Uppsala, Sörmland, Mälardalen; 5 = elsewhere in Sweden; 0 = outside Sweden |
| **Transport domain fit** | 15 | 15 = domain matches DriverNord taxonomy (CE + YKB-required role); 10 = C or D license roles; 5 = mixed or unclear; 0 = no match |
| **Decision-maker accessibility** | 15 | 15 = owner/VD directly contactable (email, LinkedIn, phone); 10 = transport manager named and reachable; 5 = HR gatekeeper only; 0 = no accessible contact found |
| **Procurement simplicity** | 15 | 15 = no supplier portal, no formal process visible; 10 = simple service agreement only; 5 = HR process, no formal panel; 0 = formal procurement portal required |
| **Urgency signal** | 10 | 10 = repeated ads (same role posted 2+ times in 4 weeks); 7 = single active ad; 3 = hiring mentioned but no active ad; 0 = no urgency signal |
| **Trust / pilot openness** | 5 | 5 = personal connection or warm introduction available; 3 = company appears innovative or tech-forward; 1 = standard traditional operator; 0 = strong signals of resistance (e.g., "we only use approved vendors") |

**Total: 100 points**

### 5.2 Score Interpretation

| Score | Classification | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|
| **70–100** | Low barrier — immediate target | Prioritize for first outreach. Pilot feasible within 30 days of first contact. |
| **45–69** | Medium barrier | Pursue after one low-barrier pilot win. Approach with reference and simple DPA in hand. |
| **20–44** | High barrier | Do not pursue until DriverNord has 3+ pilot wins and documented results. |
| **0–19** | Enterprise-only / long-term | Research only. No outreach until formal sales process and legal documentation are complete. |

### 5.3 Opportunity Score (separate from feasibility)

The pilot feasibility score measures ease of entry. The opportunity score measures upside. A company may have a high feasibility score (easy to reach) but low opportunity (one driver, small fleet). Track both.

**Opportunity score dimensions (simple, not scored):**

| Signal | Indicator |
|---|---|
| Fleet size | >20 trucks = meaningful hiring volume |
| Recurring driver need | Multiple hires per year |
| Domain breadth | Multiple transport types = multiple future needs |
| Growth signal | New contracts, expanded routes mentioned in news or LinkedIn |
| Referral potential | Well-connected in regional transport community |

## 7. First 50 Company Collection Method

### 7.1 Where to Search

**Primary sources (check in this order):**

1. **Arbetsförmedlingen (arbetsformedlingen.se/platsbanken)** — Sweden's public job board. All regulated employers must post here. Search for active driver ads. Filter by: region Stockholm, occupation "Lastbilsförare" / "Chaufför" / "Förare". Free and comprehensive.

2. **Blocket Jobb (jobb.blocket.se)** — Swedish classifieds jobs. Many SMEs and small operators post here directly. Good for smaller companies that do not use formal ATS.

3. **LinkedIn Jobs** — Filter by: location Stockholm, keywords "CE chaufför" / "lastbilschaufför" / "CE-körkort". LinkedIn also reveals company size, the posting recruiter, and sometimes direct contact.

4. **Company career pages directly** — After identifying a company from the above sources, check their own website for a "Lediga jobb" or "Karriär" section. Direct career pages often have more role detail than aggregators.

5. **Åkeriföretagen (akeri.se)** — The Swedish road transport industry association. Member directory lists transport operators by region. Many members are SMEs. Stockholm region filter gives a direct list of potential targets.

6. **Google Maps / local search** — Search "åkeri Stockholm", "transportföretag Stockholm", "logistics company Stockholm". Review company sites found. Cross-reference with job ads.

7. **Google search** — "CE-chaufför sökes Stockholm", "chaufför sökes Stockholm", "lastbilsförare sökes" — surfaces company-direct ads not on aggregators.

### 7.2 Keywords to Use

**Swedish (primary):**
- `CE-chaufför sökes`
- `lastbilschaufför Stockholm`
- `C-körkort chaufför`
- `chaufför kylfrys`
- `chaufför distribution Stockholm`
- `CE-körkort sökes`
- `YKB chaufför`
- `förare sökes Stockholm`
- `tippbil chaufför`
- `tankbil chaufför`
- `ADR-chaufför`
- `fjärrkörning chaufför`

**License class identifiers in job ads (what to look for):**
- `CE-körkort` — CE license (largest truck + trailer, highest value)
- `C-körkort` — C license (rigid truck, medium value)
- `D-körkort` — D license (bus segment; outside DriverNord's primary focus)
- `YKB` — Professional competence certificate. Presence = professional commercial driver
- `förarkort` — Driver card (tachograph). Presence = CE/C professional role
- `ADR` — Hazardous materials certification (additional to YKB)

### 7.3 Qualifying a Company (30-second scan)

For each company found in job ads, assess in order:

1. **License class:** Does the ad mention CE or C? If only B license mentioned, discard.
2. **YKB:** Is YKB mentioned? If yes, strong match signal. If not mentioned, still possibly valid — many ads omit it even when required.
3. **Location:** Is the role in Stockholm or nearby region? If Norrland or far south, lower regional score.
4. **Company size signal:** Is the job posted via the company directly, or via a staffing agency? If via a staffing agency (e.g., "this role is for our client…"), note the staffing agency as a potential agency-model target (Segment D), not the underlying client.
5. **Urgency:** Is this a repeat posting? Search company name on Arbetsförmedlingen — check date history.
6. **Decision-maker:** Can you find an owner, VD, or transport manager name on the company website or LinkedIn? If not, decision-maker access is unknown.

### 7.4 What to Record Immediately

For each qualified company, record the minimum viable set:
- Company name + website
- Job ad URL (direct link — ads expire)
- License class(es) mentioned
- YKB mentioned: yes / no
- City / region
- Company size (estimate from website, Allabolag.se)
- Decision-maker name and role (if findable)
- Direct contact (email, phone, LinkedIn)

Do not invest in full scoring until minimum viable set is recorded for all 50.

### 7.5 How to Detect Repeated Driver Hiring

1. Search company name on Arbetsförmedlingen.
2. Filter by: all dates (not just active).
3. Look for multiple ads with similar titles in the last 60–90 days.
4. Repeated posting of the same role = unfilled need = elevated urgency signal = score +10 in urgency dimension.

### 7.6 How to Avoid Wasting Time on Inaccessible Enterprise Accounts

Apply this filter before investing time in research:

| Signal | Action |
|---|---|
| Company website has "Leverantörer" or "Supplier" page with registration form | Deprioritize — likely formal procurement |
| Job ad goes to a named ATS portal (Workday, SuccessFactors, Teamtailor, Recruitee) with no direct company contact | Note as mid-size/enterprise signal; check for local contact anyway |
| Company has 500+ employees on LinkedIn | Flag as segment C or F; apply enterprise caution |
| Job ad posted by a staffing agency (not the company) | Record the agency; reach company indirectly |
| Job ad mentions "kollektivavtalet" or "union membership" prominently | Standard for larger employers; not disqualifying but signals HR involvement |
| No company website found, or site is under construction | Often micro SME; proceed but investigate before contacting |

## 8. Entry Strategy

### 8.1 Core Positioning

DriverNord must never be positioned as a staffing agency. The first thing said in any initial contact should distinguish clearly.

**Opening statement (English reference version):**

> "DriverNord is not a bemanning company. We build a qualified driver shortlist matched to your specific needs — license, YKB, domain, availability. You decide who to contact. We do not employ the drivers. We make the matching faster and the shortlist better."

**Why this matters:** Every transport company in Sweden already has a relationship with at least one staffing agency. If DriverNord is perceived as another one, the conversation immediately shifts to: price per hour, fill-rate guarantee, kollektivavtal, and employment liability — all of which DriverNord cannot currently offer. The correct frame is: acquisition infrastructure, not staffing.

### 8.2 Pilot Offer Structure

The first pilot offer should be zero-risk to the company. The goal is to generate proof, not revenue.

**Recommended first-pilot terms:**

1. Company provides one open driver need (license required, domain, region, shift type, urgency).
2. DriverNord runs the matching engine against current driver pool and delivers a shortlist within 5 business days.
3. Shortlist includes: anonymized driver profiles (license, YKB, domain, availability, score) — no PII shared before a simple DPA is signed.
4. Company reviews shortlist. If they want to contact a driver, they confirm in writing (email is sufficient).
5. DriverNord facilitates the contact (suggests message, human approves, driver is contacted via SMS or operator contacts directly).
6. Fee structure: free for first pilot. If a driver is successfully placed, discuss a referral or success fee for future engagements.

**Written pilot agreement (minimum):**
- Scope of the pilot (one need, one shortlist)
- Data handling (DriverNord processes driver profiles; company receives anonymized shortlist until DPA signed)
- No placement guarantee
- Permission to reference the company by name (or anonymized) in future materials
- Simple exit: either party can stop at any time

### 8.3 Approach Sequence

For SME targets (priority 1):

1. **Research** — Identify company, decision-maker, and driver need. Score on pilot feasibility model.
2. **Direct contact** — Email or LinkedIn message to owner/transport manager. Short, specific, no buzzwords.
3. **First response handling** — If they ask "are you a staffing agency?", address immediately. If they ask "how much does it cost?", defer to pilot scope discussion.
4. **Meeting or call** — 20 minutes. Explain the shortlist concept. Show how the matching works (screenshot of cockpit if useful). Propose the zero-risk pilot.
5. **Pilot agreement** — Email agreement is sufficient at SME level. Simple DPA if they raise data concerns.
6. **Pilot execution** — Company provides need. DriverNord delivers shortlist. Contact facilitated.
7. **Reference conversation** — After pilot, ask for permission to reference them. This is the product of the pilot.

### 8.4 What to Avoid

| Action | Why to avoid |
|---|---|
| Cold calling without research | Wastes time; no way to tailor the approach |
| Leading with "AI" or "autonomous agents" | Raises skepticism; sounds like tech hype to a transport operator |
| Promising fill rates or hiring guarantees | DriverNord cannot deliver this yet |
| Approaching HR first at mid-size companies | HR is a gatekeeper; approach operations instead |
| Sending a long PDF or slide deck as a first message | Transport operators will not read it; short, concrete, specific |
| Discussing pricing in detail before pilot value is demonstrated | Price conversations before value = race to the bottom |
| Claiming a large driver pool before the pool is real | Dishonest; verifiable; destroys trust immediately if checked |

## 9. Recommended Research Priority

The following order is strict. Do not skip ahead.

### Priority 1 — Stockholm-area SMEs with visible CE/YKB needs

**Who:** Companies with 5–50 trucks, owner-operated or single transport manager, active CE job ad in or around Stockholm.

**Target count for initial research:** 15–20 companies

**Why first:** Fastest path to a signed pilot agreement. Lowest barrier. No procurement. Decision in one conversation.

**What to look for:** CE-körkort + YKB in job ad. Company headquartered within 50km of Stockholm central. Owner or transport manager findable on company website or LinkedIn. No staffing portal. No "Leverantörer" page.

---

### Priority 2 — Regional transport operators posting driver ads (repeat posters especially)

**Who:** Companies with 50–200 trucks, regional footprint, transport manager as decision-maker. Bonus: repeated driver ads in last 60 days.

**Target count for initial research:** 15–20 companies

**Why second:** Larger volume per pilot. More demonstrable results. Still accessible without formal procurement.

**What to look for:** Multiple driver ads in 60-day window. Regional operator (not national brand). Transport manager named on website or LinkedIn.

---

### Priority 3 — Companies currently using staffing agencies for driver acquisition

**Who:** Any company from priority 1 or 2 that is identifiable as a bemanning customer. Also: transport-specialist staffing agencies themselves (Rekryteringsgruppen, Simplex, Submit) as potential agency-model B2B partners.

**Target count for initial research:** 5–10 companies (direct) + 3–5 agencies (partnership track)

**Why third:** These actors understand the market. They may be receptive to a pre-qualified pool or a partnership model. The agency partnership track (DriverNord as driver pool supplier to agencies) is a parallel path that bypasses employer-direct complexity entirely.

---

### Priority 4 — Mid-size logistics operators (200–1,000 employees)

**Who:** Companies with structured HR but still transport-operations-led. Examples: regional cold-chain operators, national distribution SMEs.

**Target count for initial research:** 5–10 companies

**Why fourth:** Higher value per pilot. More process. Requires at least one SME reference before approaching.

**What to look for:** Transport manager or operations director accessible. No formal procurement portal. Some HR function, but not HR-driven hiring process.

---

### Priority 5 — Large enterprise groups as benchmark only

**Who:** PostNord, DHL, Bring, DB Schenker, and equivalent national/international groups.

**Target count for initial research:** 4 companies (named above)

**Research output:** Supplier requirements documentation, procurement process timeline, entry path identification (regional depot contacts), GDPR requirements summary.

**No outreach.** Research only until 3+ pilot wins and documented results exist.

## 10. Next Steps After Framework

This framework defines how to think about the B2B market. The next tasks execute against it.

### Step 1 — Create research sheet (immediate)

Create the actual spreadsheet using the schema defined in Section 6. Set up columns. Create a validation dropdown for `segment`, `barrier_level`, `urgency_signal`, `decision_maker_role`, and `research_status`. This is the operational tool for everything that follows.

**Format:** Google Sheets or Excel. Not a database. The goal is fast manual entry during research, not structured querying.

### Step 2 — Collect first 50 companies (1–3 days)

Using the sources and keywords in Section 7, identify and record 50 Stockholm-area transport companies in the research sheet. Minimum viable data only: name, website, job ad URL, license mentioned, YKB mentioned, city, size estimate, decision-maker if found.

Do not score during collection. Speed is the goal for this step.

**Target composition of first 50:**
- ~20 SMEs (Priority 1)
- ~15 regional operators (Priority 2)
- ~10 repeat posters or bemanning users (Priority 3)
- ~5 mid-size logistics (Priority 4)

### Step 3 — Score and rank all 50 (1 day)

Apply the pilot feasibility score (Section 5) to all 50 companies. Identify the top 10 by score. These become the first outreach candidates.

For the top 10: complete all research sheet columns, including decision-maker name, direct contact, barrier notes, and recommended entry point.

### Step 4 — Prepare outreach scripts (1 day)

Write 3 short email/LinkedIn templates:
- Template A: Owner/VD at SME transport company (no prior contact)
- Template B: Transport manager at regional operator (no prior contact)
- Template C: Agency partnership inquiry (staffing agency as potential partner)

Each template: maximum 5 sentences. Subject line in Swedish. No PDF attachment. One clear call to action (book a 20-minute call).

### Step 5 — Begin outreach to top 10 (rolling)

Contact the top 10 companies from the scored list, in order. Track responses in the research sheet. Iterate on outreach templates based on response data.

**Success criteria for Phase 1.5:** At least 1 pilot agreement signed with a company from Priority 1 or 2. This is the gate to Phase 2 (company intake form development).
