## Consent Language Analysis

The consent text used in the `/chat` flow is defined in `driver-consent-language-v1.md` and implemented in `applyStep.ts`.

**Key elements the consent text should contain (per GDPR Art. 13):**
1. Identity of the data controller (DriverNord, org number)
2. Purpose of data processing
3. Legal basis (Art. 6(1)(a) — consent)
4. Data recipients (transport companies — described in consent scope)
5. Retention period
6. Rights of the data subject (access, erasure, portability, objection)
7. Right to withdraw consent
8. Right to lodge complaint with IMY

**Assessment:** Without reading the actual consent text in the source file, it is not possible to confirm all elements are present. The lawyer review brief should specifically request confirmation that all Art. 13 elements are present in the consent step text.

## Data Retention

**Not defined anywhere in documentation.** GDPR Art. 5(1)(e) requires data to be kept "no longer than necessary." DriverNord must define:
- How long anonymous chat sessions are retained (`anonymous_dropoff` status)
- How long disqualified driver records are retained
- How long `ready_for_ingestion` driver records are retained after matching is complete
- How long `system_actions` audit logs are retained

**This is a gap in the legal documentation.** Add a data retention schedule.

## Swedish Legal Specifics

### Marknadsföringslagen (MFL)

**Key requirements for DriverNord:**
- No misleading commercial communications
- No false claims about driver counts, company counts, or success rates
- Commercial communications must be identifiable as such
- "Free for drivers" claim is accurate and can be used

**Status:** The Creative Agent's `approval-gates-and-risk-rules.md` addresses MFL correctly. The legal documentation (`marketing-and-ads-compliance-audit.md`) provides good analysis. This is the best-documented legal area.

### Lagen om privat arbetsförmedling (LPAf)

DriverNord provides an introduction/matching service and **is not** a staffing agency (bemanningsföretag). The `driverNord-model-classification-analysis.md` concludes this correctly.

**Risk:** If any communications imply DriverNord employs, manages, or directs drivers' work, this could trigger staffing agency regulations. The content rules in `approval-gates-and-risk-rules.md` correctly prohibit this language.

### Lagen om elektronisk kommunikation (EkomL)

**The STOPP opt-out requirement is legally mandated.** When a driver sends "STOPP" by SMS, the system must immediately cease SMS communications to that number. This is not optional.

**Current state:** The STOPP requirement is documented in the human approval gates (`LO-01`) as a `LOG ONLY` automation. But the code implementation is MISSING — there is no inbound SMS webhook handler. This must be built before SMS goes live.

### Swedish employer liability

If DriverNord introduces a driver to a company and the driver is subsequently injured at work, the question of liability depends entirely on the employment relationship. Since DriverNord is not the employer, liability rests with the company. The service agreement must clearly state this. **The service agreement template needs lawyer review to confirm this limitation of liability holds under Swedish law.**

## What Must Happen Before Profile Sharing

A strict sequence of legal prerequisites must be met before any driver profile is shared with any company:

1. ✗ Migration 013 applied to production (consent capture working)
2. ✗ Privacy policy reviewed and confirmed by Swedish lawyer
3. ✗ Consent text confirmed by Swedish lawyer
4. ✗ DPA template reviewed and confirmed by Swedish lawyer
5. ✗ Executed DPA signed with the specific company before any sharing
6. ✗ Service agreement reviewed and confirmed by Swedish lawyer
7. ✗ Data retention schedule defined and documented
8. ✓ Driver consent captured (implemented, pending production migration)

**Zero of 8 prerequisites are complete.**
