# Recommended V1 Target Persona

**Date:** 2026-05-18
**Format:** Strategic memo
**Author perspective:** Transport sector insider + performance marketing practitioner

---

## The Recommendation

**Lead with Marcus. Win with Johan.**

If you only have one sentence of strategic guidance for V1: run Marcus (P2) ads to fill the registration pipeline, and spend the first week of Facebook group presence building the credibility that will eventually convert Johan (P1) to your highest-value referral source.

These are not competing strategies. They are the same strategy executed on two timelines.

---

## Single Strongest First Persona: Marcus (P2 — Missnöjde Marcus)

### Who he is

An employed CE or C driver, 27–50, working in Stockholm or Mälardalen. He's not actively job-hunting. His situation is "fine" — not great, not terrible. He has a job, he knows his routes, he's done his time with bemanningsbolag and survived. He checks his phone Sunday evenings and thinks about the week ahead with mild dread.

He's not looking for a revolution. He's looking for permission to look without admitting he's looking.

### Why Marcus first

**Volume.** There are 12,000–18,000 employed CE/C drivers in the target geography. If 35–40% are in a state of latent dissatisfaction (conservative estimate based on Transportavtalet deviation surveys and Facebook group complaint patterns), that's 4,000–7,000 reachable leads. DriverNord needs 50–100 qualified registrations to test the matching mechanics. Marcus is the fastest path to that number.

**Conversion economics.** Marcus converts at 2x the rate of Johan on cold paid media. He has lower skepticism because he's not being sold a business solution — he's being offered an escape hatch with minimal commitment required. "Register in 3 minutes. We'll contact you only if there's something relevant." For someone not actively looking, this is almost zero-risk.

**Platform fit.** Marcus is findable on Facebook. He's 27–50, he uses Facebook more than Instagram, he's in transport interest clusters, and he is most reachable at the exact moment when his dissatisfaction peaks: Sunday evening between 19:00 and 22:00. This is a targeting advantage that almost no other demographic has — a predictable emotional window.

**Lead quality is sufficient.** Marcus at a 7/10 lead quality score is acceptable for V1. DriverNord's clients (transport companies) will be testing the service too. A strong pool of employed CE drivers with valid YKB and real Stockholm availability is exactly the supply that transport companies need and will pay to access.

### Why not Johan first (despite being the premium persona)

Johan (P1) is the highest-quality lead. But he requires 3–5 touchpoints before converting, has a high trust threshold, and his primary trust signals (peer recommendation, group credibility, genuine understanding of enskild firma contract terms) take weeks to build. Running paid ads to Johan cold in V1 will produce expensive low-conversion results.

Johan converts when the conditions are right. Build those conditions over 60–90 days while Marcus fills the pipeline.

## Single Strongest First Acquisition Angle

### The Sunday Evening Frame

**Ad opening:** `"Det är söndag kväll. Imorgon tillbaka till samma sak. Det finns ett alternativ."`

**Why this is the strongest single angle:**

1. **Timing precision.** The ad runs Sunday 19:00–22:00. He reads it at the exact moment his resistance is lowest and his Monday-morning dread is highest. The timing IS part of the mechanism — do not run this ad on weekday mornings.

2. **No false promise.** "Det finns ett alternativ" does not claim the alternative is better, guaranteed, or available right now. It only names the possibility. This is Swedish-appropriate restraint — it doesn't oversell, which means it doesn't trigger cynicism.

3. **Status quo override.** By naming his Sunday night feeling exactly, the ad bypasses the rational objection ("I'm not looking for a job") because it isn't framing itself as a job ad. It's framing itself as a mirror. And he's nodding.

4. **CTA is maximal autonomy:** "3 minuter. Inget CV. Vi hör av oss bara vid match." This is the most frictionless possible CTA for someone who is not actively seeking. The lowest commitment frame in the market.

**Full ad copy:**
> "Det är söndag kväll. Imorgon tillbaka till samma sak. Det finns ett alternativ.
>
> Registrera dig på DriverNord. Ange vad du kör, när du är tillgänglig och vad som är viktigt för dig. Vi hör av oss om vi hittar nåt som faktiskt passar — annars händer ingenting.
>
> 3 minuter. Inget CV. Din profil delas inte utan ditt godkännande."

**Format:** Static image. Neutral dark background, white text only — the austerity signals that this is not a cheerful recruitment ad. Alternatively: short video (10 seconds) with text on screen only, no voiceover. Silent-scroll optimized.

## Recommended First Meta Campaign Emotional Strategy

### The core emotional position: Permission-Granting

The campaign's emotional job is not to excite Marcus. It is to give him permission to act without risking anything.

Swedish employed drivers have a strong cultural norm around not appearing disloyal or opportunistic. Actively seeking a new job while employed feels like a betrayal of the current employer — even if the current employer doesn't deserve that loyalty. Marcus has internalized this.

The campaign must externalize his permission:
- "Du söker inte aktivt. Du finns bara i rätt databas."
- "Du behöver inte göra nåt mer än detta."
- "Vi hör av oss bara om det finns nåt relevant."

Every piece of copy must reinforce that registration is not job-seeking. It is information-sharing with a service that respects his autonomy. He can register and nothing changes — unless something better comes along and he chooses it.

### The secondary emotional position: You Are Worth More Than You're Getting

Run loss aversion as the secondary angle. Not as a main campaign — as a retargeting follow-up.

After the Sunday evening touch (awareness), retarget with:
`"Vet du vad CE-förare faktiskt tjänar i Stockholm just nu?"`

This is the moment he has already seen DriverNord once. The question isn't threatening his employer — it's asking him to evaluate a factual reality. Swedish cultural norms allow information-seeking without disloyalty. Loss aversion activates naturally when the answer might be "I'm below market."

## Recommended First Visual Direction

### The Visual Rule: Honest and Unglamorous

Every ad for Marcus should look like it was made by someone who has been inside a truck terminal at 05:30 on a November morning — not someone who sells recruitment services.

**Photography:**
- Real trucks in recognizable Stockholm locations: E4 stretch near Jordbro, Södertälje terminal, Hagalund industrial area, Rosersberg logistics park
- No golden hour glamour shots. Overcast Nordic light. Morning cold. This is real.
- If there's a person: worn work jacket, realistic age (35–45), looking at a phone or a route, not smiling at camera
- If no person: the truck cab from the driver's perspective (hands on wheel, highway ahead) — intimate, first-person

**Text-forward creative:**
The strongest creative for Marcus is often text-only on a dark background. It signals serious content, not advertising. A Facebook ad that looks like a text message from an industry insider is more credible than one that looks designed.

**What to avoid:**
- Stock photos of smiling truck drivers
- Clean white studio backgrounds
- Polished brand fonts
- Green checkmarks and bullet points
- Anything that looks like it was designed by a marketing agency

**The production standard:**
Good enough to be readable. Rough enough to be authentic. Mobile first: every ad must be designed for a 390px viewport, not a 1440px desktop.

## The Single Biggest Mistake to Avoid

**Do not launch paid ads before the consent infrastructure is airtight.**

This is the one mistake that kills DriverNord before it starts.

The scenario: Marcus registers. His phone number is in the system. Before the consent flow is tested and verified, something breaks — an email goes out prematurely, a company somehow gets access, or Marcus gets contacted by DriverNord in a way that feels pushy. He posts in "Lastbilsförare i Sverige" (50,000+ members): "Registrerade mig på DriverNord och fick direkt blah blah."

That post gets 200 comments and 80 shares within 48 hours. The thread anchors in Google results for "DriverNord" for two years. The brand is done before the first placement.

Swedish transport drivers are highly connected in online communities and they share experiences rapidly. The upside of this is: good experiences also spread fast. But the downside is catastrophic for a trust violation.

**The minimum viable trust infrastructure before any paid ad runs:**
1. Registration flow tested end-to-end — consent step confirmed working
2. No automatic data sharing to any company in the system
3. Post-registration experience confirmed: one confirmation only, no follow-up until there is a genuine match
4. Founder has personally tested the full registration as a fake driver
5. A response protocol exists if something goes wrong (what to say in the Facebook thread if it happens)

**Secondary mistake to avoid:** Launching the Johan (P1) campaign cold, without group presence established first. Johan will investigate DriverNord before registering. If there's no group history, no founder presence, no organic mentions — he'll conclude it's another platform that will disappear in six months, and he'll be right to do so.

## The Final Framing

DriverNord's market position is not "better job board." It is "the service that treats drivers like professionals."

Every staffing agency in Sweden treats drivers as inventory. Every job board treats them as applicants. DriverNord's differentiation — the only differentiation that matters at V1 — is consent-first, specialist-aware, zero-pressure matching.

Marcus doesn't need to believe DriverNord will change his life. He needs to believe it won't waste his time or sell his number. If you deliver on those two promises for the first 50 registrants, the word of mouth does the rest.

The reputation DriverNord builds in the first 90 days is the asset that determines whether this becomes a real business. It cannot be bought — only earned.

Start slow. Start honest. Start Sunday evenings.
