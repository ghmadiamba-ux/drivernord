# Persona 2 — "Missnöjde Marcus" — The Dissatisfied Employed Driver

**Priority:** V1 SECONDARY — Highest volume segment. Core of any paid campaign.
**License:** CE or C (CE primary for best lead quality)
**Volume in Stockholm/Mälardalen:** ~12,000–18,000 employed CE/C drivers — of which an estimated 35–40% are in some degree of dissatisfaction at any given time
**Acquisition difficulty:** MEDIUM — not skeptical of the concept, skeptical of commitment
**Lead quality:** MEDIUM-HIGH — good leads, slower to convert

---

## 1. Demographic Reality

**Age:** 27–50. Peak segment: 32–45. Broader age range than Persona 1 — includes younger drivers who started CE recently and older ones who've been at the same company for 10 years.
**Gender:** 88–92% male. Slightly more diverse in C license segment (more women in distribution/last-mile).
**Region:** Anywhere in Stockholm/Mälardalen that has transport employers: Jordbro, Hammarby, Lunda industrial area, Enköping, Strängnäs, Örebro fringe, Södertälje, Upplands Bro.
**Experience:** 4–20 years. CE: 5+ years. C: 3+ years.
**Employment type:** Employed (either directly at a company or through a bemanningsbolag). Full-time. Covered by Transportavtalet.
**Salary reality:**
- Transportavtalet 2024/2025 base for CE in Stockholm region: approximately 28,500–32,000 SEK/month base
- With OB (ob-ersättning for nights/weekends) and overtime: 32,000–38,000 SEK gross
- Marcus suspects he may be at the low end of market — he's right about 60% of the time

---

## 2. Professional Reality

### A Typical Week

Marcus drives for a mid-sized transport company in Södertälje — bulk goods, cement and gravel for construction sites around Stockholm. Five-day week, early shifts. 05:30 start, 15:00–16:00 finish. The work is fine. The management is not. His team leader changes his route schedule at the last minute regularly ("han respekterar inte att jag har ungar"). His salary hasn't moved in 16 months despite a conversation in September about a raise that never materialized.

Or: Marcus works for a bemanning company (Proffice Transport, Randstad Staffing, one of the smaller local ones) and is placed at DHL Södertälje distribution. He doesn't have a permanent contract with DHL — just rolling 3-month extensions. He gets the work nobody else wants. The terminal manager treats him differently than the direct employees. He earns 167 SEK/hour — the bemanningsbolag charges DHL 210 SEK/hour. He did the math once. Didn't like it.

### Current Situation
Marcus is not actively searching for a new job. He's in the comfortable-enough zone: the job pays the bills, he knows the routes, he doesn't want the friction of change. But there's a background dissatisfaction that never fully goes away.

What tips him into active consideration:
- A specific frustration moment (bad schedule, pay dispute, colleague treated badly)
- A colleague mentions changing companies and getting a significant raise
- He reads something in a Facebook group about what competitors pay
- YKB renewal prompts him to think about his professional standing more broadly

### Relationship with Current Employer
Complex. He doesn't hate the company — he's invested years. But:
- Loyalty is not being reciprocated
- He feels replaceable, not valued
- Salary transparency is nonexistent (he doesn't know what colleagues earn)
- The collective agreement protects him legally but not interpersonally

### YKB Situation
Valid (most likely — he couldn't drive without it). If it expires in next 12–18 months, he's probably thinking about it. His employer may or may not pay for renewal training — this is a source of tension.

### What He Knows About the Market
Less than Johan (Persona 1). He's been at his company for 4+ years and hasn't tested the market recently. His reference points for salary and conditions are slightly outdated. He thinks changing jobs is harder than it is (recency bias toward his last job search, which was difficult).

## 3. Digital Behavior

### Facebook
Marcus uses Facebook more casually than Johan — it's entertainment + community, not just professional networking. He's in:
- "Lastbilsförare i Sverige" (lurks, occasionally comments)
- Regional community groups
- General Swedish humor groups, family groups
- **He sees transport-related content but it blends with everything else**

His Facebook behavior during work hours: scrolling during the loading dock wait. On his phone. Quickly. 5 minutes max per session. Mostly consuming, rarely commenting. **He is a thumb-stopper target** — not a reader. The hook needs to work in the first 2 words.

**Best times to reach him:** 
- 05:30–06:30 (pre-shift, coffee, phone in hand)
- 10:00–11:30 (lunch break / wait time)
- 15:30–17:30 (post-shift wind-down)
- Sunday evening 19:00–22:00 (peak dissatisfaction + openness time — thinking about Monday)

### Instagram
More than Johan — especially for under-40. Follows entertainment, humor, truck content. More visually engaged. Instagram Stories/Reels is a viable reach channel for this segment.

### TikTok
For under-35 Marcus: real chance he's on TikTok. Swedish truck content exists and grows. "Dags & Dogge"-style driving humor, truck culture, road life vlogs. V2 opportunity.

### Job Search Behavior
1. **Passive** — sees job ads on Facebook or Platsbanken when browsing, occasionally clicks
2. **Network** — if and when he decides to look: calls ex-colleagues first
3. **Platsbanken** — passive browsing but doesn't apply unless motivated
4. He has NOT registered on multiple job platforms — too passive

## 4. Emotional Profile

### Top 3 Frustrations

**1. "Lönen rör sig aldrig."**
His salary has stagnated. He knows Transportavtalet guarantees certain minimum increases but somehow they never seem to amount to much. He suspects (correctly, often) that the company is interpreting the agreement minimally. When he brings it up with his manager, he gets vague promises.

**2. "Jag känner mig inte sedd."**
The company knows his name, his route, and that he shows up. It does not know that he's good at HIAB lifts, that he handled a tricky delivery situation last winter without incident, or that he trained the new guy. Professional invisibility. He's a number, not a professional.

**3. "Schemat ändras alltid i sista minuten."**
Last-minute schedule changes with no compensation, no apology, no acknowledgment of inconvenience. This is the #1 immediate trigger for resentment. It signals that his time doesn't matter.

### Top 3 Fears

**1. "Vad händer om det nya stället är sämre?"**
He's heard stories — colleagues who left for "better" and came back worse. Job change is a leap of faith into unknown conditions. He needs partial certainty before he'll commit.

**2. "Tänk om det blir strul med semesterrätt eller pensionen om jag byter?"**
Practical fears about losing accrued benefits, pension contributions, vacation entitlements. Real concern among 35+ employed drivers.

**3. "Tänk om jag ringer nåt ställe och sen hör de av sig jämt?"**
Exactly the bemanningsbolag pattern he wants to avoid — giving out contact details and then being harassed. His phone number is guarded.

### What He Secretly Wants

He wants **confirmation that he is worth more than he is being paid**, presented in a form that lets him act on it without the social embarrassment of being seen to job-hunt. He wants a "passive escape hatch" — register somewhere, see if anything comes up, switch if it's clearly better, stay if nothing materializes.

DriverNord is exactly this. The difficulty: convincing him the escape hatch is real and not another recruiter's trap.

### Hidden Motivations
- Wants his expertise recognized and rewarded — not by his current employer (he's given up on that) but by the market
- Wants to feel that changing is his choice, not desperation
- Mild career curiosity — is he missing out? Could he earn more elsewhere?
- Some identity drift: has been at this company long enough that leaving feels disloyal, even though they haven't been loyal to him

### Trust Triggers
- Low commitment ("you don't have to do anything after registering")
- Consent model that means no cold calls ("we contact you only when there's a real match")
- Free for drivers (no financial risk)
- Company-pays model makes sense to him (he understands who benefits)
- Time clarity ("3 minutes" is believable and respectful of his time)
- No CV required (removes biggest effort objection)

### Skepticism Triggers
- "This sounds like a bemanningsbolag" — will immediately deflate interest
- Any push toward immediate commitment
- Vague language about how the matching works
- Over-promise: "hundreds of companies" or "guaranteed better pay"
- Requirement to share more data than seems necessary

## 5. Language Map

### He Says
- "Kör lastbil" — drives truck (not CE specifically in casual speech)
- "Min rutt" — my route
- "Firman" — the company (informal for his employer)
- "Lönesamtalet" — the salary discussion (often said with a sigh)
- "Ob-et" — unsocial hours supplement
- "Det är lite si och så" — it's so-so (Swedish understatement for "it's not great")
- "Kör för [company]" — works for [company] (not "employed by")
- "Det är juste" — that's fair / that makes sense (expression of approval)
- "Inte värt besväret" — not worth the hassle (his most common rationalization for not changing)
- "Vi får se" — we'll see (his default response to new opportunities)

### Words He Hates in Job Ads
Same as Persona 1, plus:
- "Du trivs med att arbeta i team" — you enjoy working in a team (irrelevant to solo driving)
- "Kommunikativ" — communicative (buzzword)
- "B-körkort krävs" — confusing when CE is required (sloppy job ads)
- "Lön enligt överenskommelse" — salary by negotiation (evasive — he wants a number)

### Phrases That Create Recognition
- "CE i Stockholmsregionen — slipp söka aktivt" — direct and specific
- "Registrera dig utan att binda upp dig" — explicit no-commitment frame
- "Vi hör av oss bara när vi har rätt match" — quiets the cold-call fear
- "Du väljer om du vill gå vidare" — control framing
- "Inget CV krävs" — removes biggest friction immediately

### How He'd Describe DriverNord to a Colleague
"Ja, jag registrerade mig. Kosta ingenting. Tog typ tre minuter. De hör av sig om de hittar nåt som passar. Vi får se."

Notably understated. Not enthusiastic. This is fine — Swedish understatement is normal, not a sign of disinterest.

## 6. Acquisition Psychology

### The Specific Moment He's Most Receptive
**Sunday evening** — the week ahead feels heavy, the thought of Monday at that company with that manager surfaces. He's scrolling Facebook, half-watching television. A DriverNord ad appears. If the hook speaks to his exact frustration, he stops. He reads. He might click.

The Sunday evening window is not metaphorical — it is a real and measurable pattern. Meta ads targeting transport workers see significantly higher engagement Sunday 19:00–22:00 because it's the peak dissatisfaction window before the work week.

### What Stops the Scroll
- **A number that makes him feel underpaid:** "CE-förare i Stockholm tjänar i snitt 35,000 SEK. Vet du vad du tjänar?" (Salary reality check — creates uncertainty)
- **A specific scenario he recognizes:** "Schema ändrat igen i sista minuten?" — naming his exact frustration
- **A simple low-commitment offer:** "3 minuter. Inget CV. Vi kontaktar dig bara vid match." — three objections answered, zero resistance created
- **A real person's face** — not a truck photo for this persona. Marcus responds more to human faces expressing relatable emotion than to truck industry imagery.

### What Prevents the Click Even After Stopping
- He goes to a website that looks corporate or complicated
- He's asked to create an account with email and password
- The first step asks for his current employer's name (feels like surveillance)
- Any form element that takes longer than expected

### CTA Style That Works
- "Se om det finns något bättre" — "see if there's something better" (curiosity, low commitment)
- "Registrera dig — det tar 3 minuter" — simple and time-bounded
- "Kör CE? Läs vad DriverNord är." — awareness step before ask

### CTA Style That Fails
- "Byt jobb nu!" — too aggressive, assumes decision he hasn't made
- "Ansök!" — wrong frame entirely

## 7. DriverNord Fit Analysis

**Why This Persona is Strategically Critical**

Marcus is the *volume* of DriverNord's supply. Not every Marcus is a premium CE specialist, but the segment contains enough valid CE drivers with YKB to build a meaningful supply-side database. The challenge: converting passive intent into registered leads, then keeping those leads warm until a match materializes.

**Expected Registration-to-Qualified-Lead Conversion:** 50–65%. Lower than Johan because a meaningful fraction will register and then go cold without completing the profile or checking back.

**Expected Lead Quality:** 6–8/10 depending on license type (CE with YKB: 8/10; C without YKB: 5/10).

**Long-Term Strategic Value:**
- High volume means a statistically significant supply base quickly
- When DriverNord successfully places Marcus, word spreads: "jag kände nån som fick nytt jobb via DriverNord"
- Marcus segment generates most of the social proof that eventually converts Johan (Persona 1)

**Risks:**
- High "ghost registration" rate (registers, never completes)
- Will disengage if not contacted within reasonable time after registration
- If first contact experience is poor (wrong specialty, cold pitch), he actively warns others

**What DriverNord Needs Before Scaling Marcus Targeting:**
- A completed /chat flow that converts well on mobile
- A working notification system for when matches appear
- At least one real transport company client in Supabase seeking CE/C drivers
- 46elks SMS configured — Marcus reads SMS, not email

## 8. Meta Campaign Implications

### Creative Direction

**Image concept:** A realistic photo of a truck driver sitting in his cab during a break, phone in hand, slight tiredness around the eyes. He's not miserable — he's just done. Ordinary. Relatable. The image caption: *"Har du koll på vad du faktiskt borde tjäna?"* — The salary reality check.

**Second image concept:** A split graphic. Left: "Sökte jobb på 5 ställen. Fick svar från 1." Right: "Registrerade mig på DriverNord på 3 minuter. Fick matchning direkt." — Simple before/after comparison.

**Video concept (15 sec):** A driver, in his cab, addresses camera. "Jag sökte inte aktivt. Registrerade mig på DriverNord. Fick ett uppdrag som passade min profil. Tog 3 minuter att registrera." Cut. No music. End card with CTA. The unremarkable delivery IS the credibility.

**Copy tone for Marcus:** Slightly softer than for Johan. Less identity-challenging, more validation-based. "It's okay to look" framing. "We don't push you" reassurance.

**Best placement:** Facebook Feed, Instagram Feed, Facebook Stories. Test Instagram Reels for under-38 segment.

**Best times:** Sunday 19:00–22:00 (primary), Weekday 05:30–07:00 (pre-shift), Friday 15:00–18:00 (end of week frustration peak).

**Meta audience targeting:**
- Location: Stockholm, Södertälje, Järfälla, Upplands Väsby, Västerås, Enköping (30km radius)
- Age: 27–50
- Interests: Trucking, logistics, Volvo Trucks, professional driver associations
- Employment: "Currently employed" behavioral signals
- Exclude: People who have engaged with bemanningsbolag content (if targetable — reduces waste)
