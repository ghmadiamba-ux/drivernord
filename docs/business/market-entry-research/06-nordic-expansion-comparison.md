## Part A: Sweden (Reference Market)

### A.1 Legal Framework

- **Staffing law:** Uthyrningslagen (2012:854); Bemanningsavtalet 2025–2027 (Kompetensföretagen × LO)
- **Employment law:** LAS (Lagen om anställningsskydd), AML (Arbetsmiljölagen)
- **24-month cap:** Uthyrningslagen §12a — after 24 months accumulated in 36, client must offer direct employment or terminate
- **Driver license/CPC authority:** Transportstyrelsen
- **YKB standard:** EU Directive 2003/59/EC + Directive 2022/2561 (updated CPC) — Swedish: Yrkeskompetensbevis
- **Collective agreement coverage:** High; Bemanningsavtalet applies to drivers employed by staffing agencies

### A.2 Market Entry Mode (Model A — Pipeline/Lead)

For the AI-pipeline model (no direct employment of drivers):
- DriverNord is NOT a staffing agency. Does not hire out employees.
- Acts as a recruitment intermediary / lead generation service.
- **Legal classification:** Rekryteringstjänst (recruitment service). Does NOT require Almega/staffing company membership.
- Revenue: placement fees + subscription
- **Regulatory requirements:** Standard Swedish company registration (AB), F-skatt, standard GDPR compliance for personal data handling.
- **Risk:** Low if clearly structured as introduction-only, not employment.

For the bemanning model (Model B — DriverNord employs drivers):
- Must comply with Bemanningsavtalet
- Must register with Almega or equivalent employer organization (or apply conditions of the agreement independently)
- 24-month rule applies immediately
- Significantly higher complexity and capital requirement

### A.3 Stockholm-First Rationale

Stockholm + Mälardalen region: ~40% of Swedish åkeri volume. Highest density of both clients (Segment A/B åkeri) and potential drivers. Most job ad activity. Most bemanning agency presence = proof that demand is there. Correct place to establish first.

## Part B: Norway

### B.1 Key Regulatory Context

#### B.1.1 The 2023 Staffing Reform (CRITICAL)

On **1 April 2023**, Norway fundamentally restructured the rules for hiring labor from staffing agencies:

**Old rule:** You could hire from agencies when the work was temporary in nature.

**New rule:** You can ONLY hire from agencies as a direct replacement for an absent permanent employee. "Temporary work" no longer justifies agency hire.

This means:
- Norwegian transport companies **cannot** use staffing agencies for general capacity or peak coverage — only to replace sick/absent permanent staff.
- Companies that relied on staffing for summer peaks or new contracts must now permanently employ those drivers.

**From January 1, 2024:** All staffing agencies operating in Norway must obtain an **authorization from Arbeidstilsynet** (Norwegian Labour Inspection Authority). Unauthorized agencies face penalties.

**Impact on DriverNord:**
- A pure staffing/bemanning model (Model B) in Norway is severely restricted by the 2023 reform.
- The **Model A pipeline/recruitment model** (permanent placement, not staffing) is UNAFFECTED by this reform and is, if anything, MORE valuable because Norwegian companies that previously used agencies for ongoing capacity must now permanently employ drivers — increasing demand for recruitment services.
- **Entry strategy:** Norway entry must be as a recruitment service (permanent placement fee), not as a staffing agency.

#### B.1.2 Equal Treatment

Norwegian Arbeidsmiljøloven §14-12a: Agency workers are entitled to equal pay and conditions as if they had been directly employed. Essentially the same as Swedish principle.

#### B.1.3 Driver License / CPC

- Norwegian authority: **Statens vegvesen**
- Professional competence certificate equivalent: **Yrkessjåførkompetansebevis (YSK)** — Norway's CPC
- Norwegian CE license is directly recognized in Sweden (and vice versa) within EU/EEA.
- Norwegian drivers have essentially the same credential structure as Swedish drivers.

#### B.1.4 Language

Norwegian bokmål is highly intelligible to Swedish speakers. A Swedish-language platform can be adapted to Norwegian with minimal translation effort (UI strings, terms and conditions, communications). Many Norwegian transport professionals speak Swedish or read it comfortably.

### B.2 Market Characteristics

| Factor | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Trucking companies | ~15,000–20,000 registered, including smaller operators |
| Driver shortage | Consistent shortage; listed in shortage occupation reports |
| Major operators | Bring Cargo, PostNord NO, Schenker NO, NTG Nordic |
| Major agencies (pre-2023) | Adecco, Manpower, smaller transport-specific agencies |
| Post-2023 agency usage | Severely restricted; recruitment service demand should have increased |
| Wage level | Higher than Sweden: NOK 35,000–45,000/month (CE + YKB equivalent) |
| Oslo market | Comparable density to Stockholm; primary entry point for Norway |

### B.3 Norway Entry Prerequisites

1. **Swedish market established** — 50+ successful placements, 200+ drivers in database
2. **Legal entity in Norway** — NUF (Norwegian branch of Swedish AB) or Norwegian AS
3. **Norwegian Arbeidstilsynet check** — confirm we are a rekrutteringstjeneste, not vikarbyråen (staffing agency)
4. **Norwegian driver community outreach** — separate driver registration campaign in Norway
5. **Norwegian language UI** — translation of core platform pages
6. **Norwegian company research targets** — separate `company_research_targets` data for Oslo/Viken region

### B.4 Norway Entry Timeline Recommendation

**Enter Norway in Year 2 (Q1–Q2 2027):**
- Ride the post-2023 reform wave — companies that previously used agencies for capacity need recruitment services NOW.
- Use Swedish proof points (50+ placements) to build trust with Norwegian clients.
- Start with Oslo region (equivalent role to Stockholm in Sweden).

## Part C: Denmark

### C.1 Key Regulatory Context

#### C.1.1 Staffing Law

Denmark has a vikarbureau (temporary work agency) structure. Key law: **Lov om vikarers retsstilling** (implementing EU Directive 2008/104/EC).

Core principle: Temporary workers (vikarer) are entitled to equal treatment — same pay and conditions as comparable permanent employees at the client company. This mirrors Swedish and Norwegian law.

**Denmark has NOT imposed the same 2023-style restrictions as Norway.** Staffing/vikarbureau work for "temporary" needs remains permitted. The Danish market is more permissive for staffing than Norway.

#### C.1.2 Collective Agreements — Transport

- **ATL (Aftale om transport og logistik)** — the sector agreement covering road freight
- **3F (Fagligt Fælles Forbund)** — the main trade union covering transport and logistics workers
- Staffing agencies in transport sector typically operate under **Vikar/bemanningsbranchen overenskomst** with 3F

#### C.1.3 Driver License / CPC

- Danish authority: **Trafikstyrelsen**
- Professional competence: **EU CPC** (Erhvervsmæssig kompetenceattest)
- Same EU directive applies; Danish CPC is recognized in Sweden and Norway.
- Danish CE license structure matches EU standard.

#### C.1.4 Language

Danish is the most linguistically distant of the three markets for a Swedish team. However:
- Most Danish business communication can be done in English or Swedish
- Danish transport professionals generally understand Swedish (especially written)
- Platform translation to Danish requires dedicated effort — more than Norway

### C.2 Market Characteristics

| Factor | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Trucking companies | ~10,000–15,000 registered transport companies |
| Driver shortage | Listed shortage occupation; EURES confirms vacancy pressure |
| Major operators | DSV, Frode Laursen, Bpost/Spring, DHL Freight DK |
| Wage level | DKK 30,000–35,000/month (~41,000–48,000 SEK equivalent) |
| Copenhagen market | Primary entry point; Denmark's equivalent of Stockholm |
| Cross-border transport | High — Copenhagen-Malmö corridor is unique; drivers already cross |

### C.3 Denmark Entry Prerequisites

1. **Swedish + Norway markets established** — 100+ placements, 500+ drivers
2. **Legal entity in Denmark** — Danish ApS or branch of Swedish AB
3. **3F/Vikarbureau compliance check** — Danish labor law counsel required
4. **Danish driver community outreach** — separate campaign
5. **Danish language UI** — full translation required
6. **Separate database** — Danish `company_research_targets`

### C.4 Denmark Entry Timeline Recommendation

**Enter Denmark in Year 3 (2028):**
- Copenhagen-Malmö corridor opportunity: Swedish drivers crossing into Denmark and vice versa creates a natural bridge.
- Lower regulatory complexity than Norway (staffing still permitted).
- Build on Nordic proof points.

## Part D: Cross-Nordic Regulatory Summary

### D.1 Key Differences That Affect DriverNord

| Issue | Sweden | Norway | Denmark |
|-------|--------|--------|---------|
| Can run staffing/bemanning for capacity? | Yes | NO (since April 2023) | Yes |
| Recruitment/placement service legal? | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| License authority | Transportstyrelsen | Statens vegvesen | Trafikstyrelsen |
| YKB/CPC equivalent name | YKB | YSK | EU CPC (erhvervsmæssig) |
| Cross-Nordic license recognition | Yes (EU/EEA mutual) | Yes | Yes |
| Equal treatment principle | Yes (uthyrningslagen) | Yes (AML §14-12a) | Yes (vikarloven) |
| Staffing company authorization needed? | No formal licensing (Almega membership) | YES (Arbeidstilsynet since 2024) | No formal licensing |
| Main trade union (transport) | Transport (GS-facket) | NTF | 3F |

### D.2 The Norway Reform — Strategic Implication

The 2023 Norwegian reform is the most important regulatory fact for DriverNord's Nordic strategy:

**Pre-reform:** Norwegian companies supplemented headcount with agency workers → agencies handled the "extra capacity" problem.

**Post-reform:** Norwegian companies must permanently employ any driver needed for more than replacement cover → they need to recruit more → demand for recruitment services has sharply increased → DriverNord enters exactly this gap.

The reform essentially **banned the competitor model (staffing) and created the market (permanent placement recruitment)** for DriverNord in Norway simultaneously.
