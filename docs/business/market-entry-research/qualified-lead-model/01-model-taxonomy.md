# 01 — Monetization Model Taxonomy for Qualified Driver Leads

**Research date:** 2026-05-12  
**Purpose:** Define and compare every monetization model available to DriverNord. Each model is evaluated on risk allocation, payment timing, fit for DriverNord, and fit for SME transport clients.

---

## Overview

Thirteen distinct revenue models exist in the recruitment and lead generation space. They differ primarily on:
1. **What triggers payment** (a lead? an application? an interview? a hire?)
2. **Who bears the risk** (buyer pays regardless of outcome, or only on success?)
3. **What counts as "qualified"** (self-certified? externally verified? tried and approved?)

---

## Model 1 — Pay-Per-Lead (PPL)

**How it works:**  
Buyer pays a fixed fee for each name/contact detail delivered, regardless of whether the lead converts into a hire. No qualification standard required beyond basic criteria (e.g., "CE license holder in Stockholm").

**Who pays:** Buyer (åkeri) pays DriverNord.  
**When payment happens:** Upon delivery of each lead.  
**What counts as "qualified":** Minimal — typically self-reported license class + location. No verification.  
**Risk allocation:** All on buyer. Leads may be inaccurate, unavailable, or unsuitable.

**Fits DriverNord?** No — low value, low trust, legally risky (delivering unverified data of private individuals).

| | SME transport client | DriverNord |
|--|--|--|
| **Pros** | Cheap per unit; no commitment | Easy to produce; low ops cost |
| **Cons** | Zero quality guarantee; wastes time | Destroys trust if lead quality is low; GDPR risk |

---

## Model 2 — Pay-Per-Qualified-Lead (PPQL)

**How it works:**  
Buyer pays for each lead that meets a verified qualification standard (e.g., confirmed CE + valid YKB + available within X weeks). DriverNord bears the verification burden; buyer only pays for leads meeting the standard.

**Who pays:** Buyer (åkeri).  
**When payment happens:** Upon delivery of a lead that passes qualification check.  
**What counts as "qualified":** Verified license class + YKB status confirmed + explicit driver consent to be presented.  
**Risk allocation:** Mixed — DriverNord verifies so quality is guaranteed; buyer still takes the risk that a qualified lead doesn't result in a hire.

**Fits DriverNord?** Yes — this is the minimum viable monetization unit. Each verified driver introduction is a PPQL sale.

| | SME transport client | DriverNord |
|--|--|--|
| **Pros** | Pays only for relevant profiles; fast value delivery | Higher margin than PPL; trust-building model |
| **Cons** | May buy 3-5 leads before finding a hire; cost adds up | Must invest in verification process |

---

## Model 3 — Pay-Per-Application (PPA)

**How it works:**  
An employer posts a job. The platform delivers candidates who have actively applied. Payment per application received (whether or not the applicant is qualified or hired).

**Who pays:** Employer/buyer.  
**When payment happens:** Per application received.  
**What counts as "qualified":** Usually not controlled — applicants self-select.  
**Risk allocation:** On buyer; quality varies wildly.

**Context:** Indeed historically used this model; discontinued their PPA offering in December 2023. The model proves buyers will pay per application but quality control is the failure point.

**Fits DriverNord?** No — passive, no verification, same problem as job boards.

| | SME transport client | DriverNord |
|--|--|--|
| **Pros** | Familiar model, easy to understand | Simple billing |
| **Cons** | High unqualified application rate; no CE/YKB filter | No differentiation from Blocket Jobb |

---

## Model 4 — Pay-Per-Interview (PPI)

**How it works:**  
Buyer pays only when a candidate attends a scheduled interview. Platform does all the sourcing, screening, and scheduling. Payment is per interview completed (or per interview that leads to a hire, depending on contract).

**Who pays:** Buyer.  
**When payment happens:** When interview is confirmed/held.  
**What counts as "qualified":** Sufficiently screened to warrant an interview — usually 1+ pre-screening call or automated assessment.  
**Risk allocation:** Risk shared — buyer pays only for interviews that pass a basic screen.

**Fits DriverNord?** Partially — workable as part of a higher-value package (e.g., we source + screen + book; you only pay for who you actually meet).

| | SME transport client | DriverNord |
|--|--|--|
| **Pros** | No payment for no-shows; quality signal | Positions DriverNord as high-touch service |
| **Cons** | Requires scheduling coordination; administrative cost | Scheduling is operational overhead |

---

## Model 5 — Pay-Per-Shortlist

**How it works:**  
Buyer pays for a packaged shortlist of 3–5 pre-screened, verified candidates ready to interview. This is distinct from individual leads — the buyer gets a curated set.

**Who pays:** Buyer.  
**When payment happens:** When shortlist is delivered.  
**What counts as "qualified":** Verified credentials + availability confirmed + driver consented to be presented to this specific client.  
**Risk allocation:** Buyer bears hire risk; DriverNord bears quality risk (shortlist must meet spec).

**Fits DriverNord?** Yes — strong product-market fit. Addresses the "send me 3 good CE drivers" pain point directly. Priced higher than single leads.

| | SME transport client | DriverNord |
|--|--|--|
| **Pros** | Saves time; guaranteed qualified pool; clear deliverable | Premium pricing; scalable; drives database usage |
| **Cons** | Upfront cost before hire; client must still do interviews | Must maintain sufficient database depth per region |

---

## Model 6 — Pay-Per-Hire / Success-Fee Recruitment (Contingency)

**How it works:**  
No payment until the candidate is hired. Fee is charged as a percentage of first-year annual salary (or a flat fee) triggered by contract signing.

**Who pays:** Buyer (åkeri).  
**When payment happens:** When driver signs employment contract (or after 30 days of employment — varies).  
**What counts as "qualified":** The hire itself — client chose to employ.  
**Risk allocation:** All on DriverNord. Zero payment if no hire occurs. Client has no financial exposure until success.

**Context:** Standard model for professional recruitment agencies (UK: 15–20% logistics; Sweden: 23–27% general, 15–20% blue-collar/logistics).

**Fits DriverNord?** Yes — but risky for DriverNord in early stage. If 0 hires happen for 2 months, DriverNord earns nothing. Best used once database and process are proven.

| | SME transport client | DriverNord |
|--|--|--|
| **Pros** | Zero risk; only pay for outcome; easy to say yes | No barrier to first sale; high ceiling per placement |
| **Cons** | No urgency to engage; client may ghost if hire takes long | Revenue uncertainty; cash flow risk |

---

## Model 7 — Retained Recruitment

**How it works:**  
Client pays a upfront retainer (30–40% of expected total fee) to commence a search, a second tranche on shortlist delivery, and a final tranche on hire. Total fee ~20–30% of first-year salary.

**Who pays:** Buyer — in tranches.  
**When payment happens:** Upfront + shortlist + hire.  
**What counts as "qualified":** Deliverable-based — each stage has a defined output.  
**Risk allocation:** Shared — client pays even if search fails (retainer is non-refundable); agency bears search effort risk.

**Fits DriverNord?** Not for Year 1. Retained search requires established trust and typically used for hard-to-fill senior or specialized roles. A CE driver search doesn't command retainer terms yet.

| | SME transport client | DriverNord |
|--|--|--|
| **Pros** | Predictable revenue for DriverNord | Serious client commitment |
| **Cons** | SME åkerier resist upfront payment; unusual for driver roles | Too complex for early-stage; not market-fit for drivers |

---

## Model 8 — Flat-Fee Recruitment

**How it works:**  
Client pays a fixed fee regardless of candidate salary. Can be structured as: (a) all upfront, (b) split upfront/success, or (c) success-only flat fee.

**Real example (Sweden):** Headscout.se — 15,000 SEK upfront + 59,900 SEK on signature = **74,900 SEK total** for general roles.

**Who pays:** Buyer.  
**When payment happens:** Split between start and hire (or all at hire).  
**What counts as "qualified":** The hire itself.  
**Risk allocation:** Depends on structure. If all-success: all risk on DriverNord. If split: partial risk sharing.

**Fits DriverNord?** Yes — particularly attractive for CE driver roles where annual salary is predictable. A flat fee removes the salary disclosure issue and gives budget predictability to clients.

| | SME transport client | DriverNord |
|--|--|--|
| **Pros** | Budget certainty; no percentage surprises; fair for low-salary hires | Simpler to sell; removes salary negotiation from pricing |
| **Cons** | Less favorable if driver salary ends up high (vs. percentage model) | Revenue capped per hire; must do volume |

---

## Model 9 — Subscription Access to Candidate Database

**How it works:**  
Client pays a monthly or annual subscription to access a searchable database of consenting candidates. Client can search, view profiles (anonymized or named), and contact drivers. No per-placement fee.

**Real example (Sweden):** Chaffis.se — **4,995 SEK/year** (unlimited access to driver database for åkerier).  
**Real example (Sweden):** Förartjänst.se — annual fee for companies, database of 8,000+ drivers.

**Who pays:** Buyer (åkeri).  
**When payment happens:** Upfront annual (or monthly).  
**What counts as "qualified":** Driver self-reported credentials (quality depends on platform's verification process).  
**Risk allocation:** All on buyer — no guarantee of hires, no support.

**Fits DriverNord?** Yes — particularly as a low-barrier entry product. Annual subscription is easy to sell; builds predictable revenue. Key differentiation vs. Chaffis/Förartjänst: DriverNord verifies credentials.

| | SME transport client | DriverNord |
|--|--|--|
| **Pros** | Predictable cost; no per-hire fee; self-service | Recurring revenue; scales with database size |
| **Cons** | No guarantee of result; requires client effort to search | Not differentiated if database is unverified |

---

## Model 10 — Recruitment-as-a-Service (RaaS)

**How it works:**  
Client pays a flat monthly subscription fee to outsource their entire recruitment function for a specific role type. DriverNord acts as the on-demand recruiter: source, screen, shortlist, coordinate. Unlimited hires per month within the subscription.

**Typical pricing (UK/Europe):** £2,000–£5,000/month for a dedicated role specialist. Claims 30–50% saving vs. traditional agency fees.

**Who pays:** Buyer.  
**When payment happens:** Monthly recurring.  
**What counts as "qualified":** Defined in the RaaS service agreement.  
**Risk allocation:** On DriverNord — must deliver consistently or client cancels.

**Fits DriverNord?** Good fit for Segment B/C (regional operators needing 5+ drivers/year). Not right for Segment A SME (too expensive for a 3-truck company).

| | SME transport client | DriverNord |
|--|--|--|
| **Pros** | Eliminates hiring overhead; predictable cost | Recurring revenue; deep client relationship |
| **Cons** | Monthly cost regardless of actual hiring activity | Operationally intensive; requires consistent output |

---

## Model 11 — Talent Marketplace Commission

**How it works:**  
A two-sided platform where drivers list themselves and companies post needs. Platform takes a commission on each match — either from the driver (percentage of wages), from the company (percentage of first contract value), or both. Examples: DrivenPeople (UK), Toptal (white collar), Bark.com (service professionals).

**Who pays:** Varies — can be employer-side, worker-side, or both.  
**When payment happens:** On engagement/contract.  
**What counts as "qualified":** Platform-defined; can be self-reported + verified.  
**Risk allocation:** Platform bears matchmaking cost; parties bear engagement risk.

**Fits DriverNord?** Medium-term fit. A marketplace commission requires two-sided critical mass. Not achievable in Year 1.

---

## Model 12 — Staffing/Bemanning Hourly Margin

**How it works:**  
DriverNord employs drivers (on Bemanningsavtalet), deploys them to client åkeri, and bills an hourly invoice rate that covers employment cost + markup. Client pays per hour worked.

**Invoice rate target:** 400–480 SEK/hour for CE + YKB.  
**Who pays:** Client (per hour).  
**When payment happens:** Monthly invoice on hours worked.  
**What counts as "qualified":** Driver is employed and credentialed by DriverNord.  
**Risk allocation:** DriverNord bears all employment risk (payroll, sick leave, bench time).

**Fits DriverNord?** See File 07 in main research series — not Year 1, selective Year 2.

## Model Comparison for DriverNord

| Model | Risk for DriverNord | Risk for client | Revenue certainty | SME fit | Recommended? |
|-------|---------------------|-----------------|-------------------|---------|--------------|
| Pay-per-lead (unverified) | Low | High | Low | Medium | No |
| **Pay-per-qualified-lead** | Medium | Medium | Medium | **High** | **Year 1 primary** |
| Pay-per-application | Low | High | Low | Low | No |
| Pay-per-interview | Medium | Low | Medium | Medium | Selective |
| **Pay-per-shortlist** | Medium | Medium-low | Medium | **High** | **Year 1 primary** |
| **Success-fee (flat)** | High | **None** | Low | **High** | **Year 1 (entry offer)** |
| **Success-fee (%)** | High | None | Low | Medium | Year 1–2 |
| Retained | Medium-high | Medium | Medium | Low | Year 2+ |
| **Flat-fee recruitment** | Medium | Medium | Medium | **High** | **Year 1** |
| **Subscription DB access** | Low | Medium | **High** | **High** | **Year 1** |
| RaaS | Medium | Low | High | Low | Year 2 |
| Marketplace commission | Medium | Medium | Medium | Medium | Year 2+ |
| Staffing hourly | High | Low | High | Low | Year 2 (selective) |

**Year 1 recommended portfolio:**  
1. **Verified driver introduction** (PPQL) — 5,000–10,000 SEK per introduction  
2. **Shortlist of 3–5 drivers** — 12,000–25,000 SEK per shortlist  
3. **Success-fee placement** — 20,000–40,000 SEK flat fee on hire  
4. **Annual subscription** — 5,000–10,000 SEK/year for ongoing database access
