### 1.3 Root Causes (Sweden)

1. **Aging workforce** — the current pool is skewing toward 55+. Retirement wave is imminent (2026–2032).
2. **Declining vocational enrollment** — multiple municipalities are cutting seats on Fordon- och transportprogrammet (vehicle and transport high school program). Signal noted by TYA in 2024.
3. **Cost and time of licensure** — CE license + YKB costs 50,000–120,000 SEK and 3–12 months of training. No personal funding mechanism exists; individuals cannot self-finance easily.
4. **Working conditions perception** — demanding hours, night work, time away from family. Low status perception vs. other vocational trades.
5. **Underrepresentation of women and immigrants** — only ~20% of transport sector workers are women (TRANSPORTochLOGISTIK.se). Non-EU license conversion and language barriers limit immigrant driver entry.
6. **Structural demand growth** — e-commerce logistics volumes keep growing, increasing demand for last-mile and distribution drivers even in economic slowdowns.

## 2. How Swedish Transport Companies Currently Find Drivers

### 2.1 Primary Channels (ranked by prevalence)

#### A. Direct job postings — Platsbanken / Blocket Jobb / Indeed / Monster SE

The most common approach for SME åkeri companies. They post on free/low-cost job boards and wait. Problems:
- Ads go unanswered for weeks; "repeated ads" is the most common urgency signal
- No pre-screening — companies waste time processing unqualified applicants
- No visibility into license validity, YKB status, or tachograph card

**Pain point for DriverNord:** "Segment E" companies (repeated ads) in our taxonomy are the highest urgency and most accessible entry point.

#### B. Employee referrals (informella nätverk)

Very common, especially in SME åkerier (Segment A). Owners personally know other owners; drivers recruit drivers. Problems:
- Pool exhausted quickly
- No structured process; relies on luck
- Informal — no written offer, sometimes no employment contract until forced

#### C. Bemannings/staffing agencies

Used primarily by larger companies (Segments B, C, D) that need to fill slots quickly and can absorb the 30–50% markup over direct employment cost. Smaller companies (Segment A) typically find the price prohibitive. The main agencies:
- Jobzone (Nordic-wide, transport specialist)
- Simplex Bemanning (Stockholm region)
- Veterankraft (senior C/CE/D + YKB)
- Rekryteringsgruppen, Submit AB, Storesupport, Workforce

**Pain point for DriverNord:** SME companies that cannot afford agency markup but also cannot fill via job boards — this is the gap.

#### D. Direct outreach to Transportstyrelsen-registered holders

Not used in practice — Transportstyrelsen register of license holders is not publicly searchable by employers.

#### E. Training school pipelines

Some larger fleets partner directly with Yrkeshögskola or Gymnasiet transport programs. Gets new-entrant drivers but takes 12–18 months lead time. Only feasible for companies with volume and planning horizon.

#### F. International recruitment (Baltic, Poland, Romania)

Growing post-2019, accelerated by COVID recovery. Primarily used by larger operators and logistics groups (Schenker, PostNord, DHL). Requires Swedish work permit or EU right to work, license conversion, YKB equivalence validation. Heavy upfront cost. Does not solve SME åkeri urgent need.

## 3. Pain Points by Company Segment

### Segment A — SME Transport Company (5–50 trucks, owner-operated)

**Typical characteristics:** Family-owned, 1–10 trucks, regional routes, owner doubles as driver or dispatcher.

| Pain | Severity (1–5) | Description |
|------|----------------|-------------|
| No systematic recruiting process | 5 | Rely entirely on word-of-mouth and Platsbanken |
| Cannot evaluate candidates for license validity | 4 | No access to Transportstyrelsen data; trust verbal claims |
| Cannot afford bemanning markup | 4 | A CE driver at 450 SEK/h via agency vs. ~320 SEK/h direct is unaffordable long-term |
| No HR staff | 5 | Owner handles everything; recruiting is a distraction from operations |
| Time-to-hire delay causes lost contracts | 4 | Missing a driver means turning down freight — direct revenue loss |
| Repeated job ad posting | 4 | Spending money on ads that yield zero qualified applicants |

**What they want:** A fast list of immediately available, verified CE/YKB-qualified drivers within their geographic area. They'll pay for this if it's cheaper than bemanning and faster than job boards.

### Segment B — Regional Transport Operator (50–200 trucks)

**Typical characteristics:** Fleet manager, some HR capacity, multi-depot, mixed freight.

| Pain | Severity (1–5) | Description |
|------|----------------|-------------|
| Volume demand — need 5–20 drivers at once | 5 | Job boards cannot deliver this at once |
| Quality inconsistency from agencies | 3 | Agency drivers vary; some lack specific equipment knowledge |
| Onboarding cost | 3 | Each new driver needs site induction, fuel card, company app training |
| Summer/peak season spikes | 5 | Need surge capacity without permanent headcount |
| Long agency contract lock-ins | 3 | Minimum volume commitments from agencies |

**What they want:** Verified bulk supply of pre-screened drivers, fast, with flexibility. Willing to pay a reasonable placement fee or per-introduction fee rather than per-hour markup.

### Segment C — Mid-size Logistics Company (200–1000 employees)

**Typical characteristics:** Has dedicated HR/recruitment. Uses multiple agencies. May have procurement policy.

| Pain | Severity (1–5) | Description |
|------|----------------|-------------|
| Procurement compliance | 3 | Must use approved vendor list; new supplier takes 3–6 months |
| Seasonal flexibility | 4 | Q4 peak, summer peak — need 10–50 extra drivers fast |
| Compliance liability | 4 | They are responsible for ensuring drivers have valid licenses; agency failure creates their liability |
| Driver retention | 4 | Good drivers leave for permanent positions; high churn via agencies |

**What they want:** Compliance-guaranteed supply with SLA, digital records of licenses/YKB for each placed driver.

### Segment D — Companies That Already Use Staffing Agencies

Currently paying bemanning markup. Open to alternatives that reduce cost while maintaining supply certainty.

### Segment E — Companies Repeatedly Posting Driver Ads

Most urgent signal. These are companies actively failing to fill a known gap. They have budget, they have intent, they just cannot convert. **Highest receptivity to DriverNord outreach.**

## 4. The Search-and-Match Gap

Current market has a structural matching failure:

```
Available drivers                     Companies needing drivers
─────────────────                     ────────────────────────
• CE/YKB certified                    • SME åkeri, 1–50 trucks
• Willing to work                     • Cannot find qualified people
• Open to new assignments             • Cannot verify credentials
• Location-flexible                   • Cannot afford agency markup
• Unknown to companies                • No systematic search tool
```

**No platform or service currently:**
1. Maintains a searchable, verified database of available CE/YKB/C+YKB drivers in Sweden
2. Pre-verifies license classes and YKB validity before presenting to clients
3. Offers a non-bemanning pricing model (lead fee / placement fee vs. per-hour margin)
4. Targets the sub-50-truck SME segment specifically

## 6. Supply-Side Summary: Available Driver Profile

Based on Transportavtalet demographics and TYA research:

- **Where qualified drivers are:** Concentrated in Stockholm, Göteborg, Malmö urban areas. Mälardalen corridor has significant density.
- **Age profile:** Heavy clustering 35–55. Very few under 25. Growing cohort over 55 who may prefer part-time or flexible assignments.
- **Employment status pool:** 
  - Permanently employed but open to change (passive)
  - Between assignments (active, short-term gap)
  - Recently laid off (active)
  - Returned from illness/leave (re-entering)
  - Working for agency but seeking better conditions (active)
- **License profile:** Most professional drivers hold at minimum C + YKB. CE is the premium combination. D (bus) is a separate pool.
- **Preferred working mode:** Most prefer stable long-term employment. A significant minority (particularly senior/older drivers) are willing to work on-call or project basis if pay compensates.
